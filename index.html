Pesto ↗

Yield: ~1 cup | Total Time: 15 min

Ingredients

Steps

  1. Toast pine nuts in a small skillet over medium heat until golden brown, about 2–3 minutes.
  2. In a food processor, add half the pine nuts, 3 cups basil, 2 garlic cloves, ½ cup cheese, and lemon zest. Process ~30 seconds.
  3. With processor running on low, slowly drizzle in ¾ cup olive oil until a smooth paste forms. Scrape down the sides.
  4. Add remaining pine nuts, basil, garlic, and cheese. Pulse until finely chopped.
  5. Transfer to a bowl and stir in remaining olive oil. Season with salt and pepper.
  6. Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days.

Toni's Crab Dip

Source: Family recipe (handwritten card) — no online link on file

Yield: 6–8 servings | Prep: 10 min | Bake: 45 min

A beloved family recipe from a handwritten card — warm, creamy baked crab dip with cream cheese, sour cream, mayo, and sharp cheddar. The paprika finish is the signature touch.

Ingredients

Steps

  1. Preheat oven to 325°F.
  2. In a blender or food processor, blend the cream cheese, sour cream, mayonnaise, lemon juice, dry mustard, and garlic until smooth.
  3. Fold in the crabmeat and ½ cup of the shredded cheddar by hand. Transfer to a serving dish.
  4. Bake at 325°F for 45 minutes.
  5. Remove from oven. Top with the remaining ½ cup cheddar and a generous sprinkle of paprika. Serve warm with crackers or crusty bread.

Notes & Tips

  • Backfin crabmeat is the sweet spot — lump works beautifully too.
  • Soften the cream cheese fully before blending or it won’t combine smoothly.
  • Can be assembled a day ahead and refrigerated unbaked — add a few extra minutes to bake time.
  • The paprika on top is the signature finishing touch. Be generous.

Classic Pimiento Cheese (Pate of the South) ↗

Yield: ~2 cups | Total Time: 15 min

No-cook, no-fuss, and endlessly versatile — sharp cheddar, mayo, and pimentos combine into a tangy, creamy spread. Fun fact: while pimiento cheese is now considered the quintessential Southern spread (often nicknamed the “pate of the South”), its earliest roots trace to New York, where cream cheese makers began experimenting with imported Spanish pimentos in the late 1800s before it migrated south.

Ingredients

Steps

  1. Grate the cheddar cheese using a box grater or food processor. Avoid pre-shredded cheese.
  2. In a large bowl, combine the grated cheese, drained pimentos, cayenne, and black pepper.
  3. Add ¾ cup mayonnaise and stir until just combined. Add more mayonnaise up to 1 cup total, to preference.
  4. Taste and adjust seasoning with salt, more cayenne, or black pepper as needed.
  5. Serve immediately at room temperature, or refrigerate up to 3 days.

Notes & Tips

  • Grate your own cheese — pre-shredded cheese won’t bind properly with the mayo.
  • Duke’s mayonnaise is the Southern standard.
  • Serve with Ritz crackers, on white bread, or use for a grilled cheese.

Baked Brie with Brown Sugar ↗

Yield: 6–8 servings | Prep: 5 min | Total Time: ~25 min

Great party food — a 5-inch brie round baked with brown sugar and butter until soft and gooey, topped with walnuts or pecans. Serve with sliced French bread. Also appears in the Thanksgiving section.

Ingredients

Steps

  1. Preheat oven to 325°F.
  2. Put the brie in a baking dish.
  3. Brush approximately 3 tbsp softened butter over the top.
  4. Sprinkle about ¼ cup brown sugar evenly over the brie.
  5. Add about ¼ cup walnuts or chopped pecans on top, whatever you have on hand.
  6. Bake for 20–25 minutes, until soft and gooey.
  7. Serve with sliced French bread.

Notes & Tips

  • Go easy on the butter — just enough to brush the surface, not drown it.
  • Great party food that comes together in minutes.
  • Also appears in: Thanksgiving section.

Old-Fashioned Beef Stew ↗

Yield: 4 servings | Total Time: ~2 hrs 30 min

A classic stick-to-your-ribs stew — beef, onions, carrots, potatoes, and red wine. Doubles or triples beautifully.

Ingredients

Steps

  1. Combine flour and pepper in a bowl, add beef, and toss to coat.
  2. Heat 3 tsp oil in a large pot. Brown beef in batches, about 5 min per batch.
  3. Remove beef. Add vinegar and wine, scraping up browned bits.
  4. Return beef to pot. Add broth and bay leaves. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a slow simmer.
  5. Cover and cook until beef is tender, about 1½ hours.
  6. Add onions and carrots. Simmer covered 10 minutes.
  7. Add potatoes and simmer until tender, about 30 minutes more.
  8. Season with salt and pepper. Serve in 4 bowls.

Slow Cooker Brisket with Caramelized Onions

Source: David's own recipe — no online link on file

Yield: 6–8 servings | Active Time: 30 min | Cook: 6–8 hrs (LOW)

The primary brisket — deeply savory, impossibly tender, built around a mountain of caramelized onions. Perfect for Passover and holidays.

Ingredients

Steps

  1. CARAMELIZE THE ONIONS: Heat olive oil in a large deep sauté pan over medium. Cook onions on medium-low, stirring frequently, until lightly caramelized, about 20 min.
  2. SEAR THE BRISKET: Pat dry, season generously with salt and pepper. Sear in a hot skillet until golden-brown crust forms on both sides. Transfer to a 6-qt slow cooker fat side up. Sprinkle with garlic.
  3. Pile caramelized onions on top and around the brisket. Whisk broth, Worcestershire, and soy sauce; pour in.
  4. COOK: LOW for 6–8 hours until very tender. Switch to WARM; rest at least 20 min.
  5. SERVE OR REFRIGERATE: Slice or shred immediately, or refrigerate overnight — flavors improve and fat is easy to skim.
  6. NEXT-DAY: Scrape away solidified fat before reheating.
  7. REHEAT: 300°F oven, covered tightly, 1 hour or until warmed through.

Notes & Tips

  • The two-step sear + slow cooker is what makes this — don’t skip the sear.
  • Make the day before: flavor deepens and fat is easy to skim.
  • Slice against the grain, using the full length of the knife.
  • Pairs perfectly with mashed potatoes, roasted carrots, and matzo for Passover.

Melt-in-Your-Mouth Oven Brisket ↗

Yield: 10–12 servings | Marinate: up to 24 hrs | Cook: 6–8 hrs | Freezer-friendly

Mila Furman's fuss-free Jewish brisket — ketchup, red wine, and chicken base slow-roasted to falling-apart perfection. Make ahead, freeze, reheat the day of.

Ingredients

Marinade

Roasting Vegetables

Steps

  1. MAKE THE MARINADE: Combine all marinade ingredients. Slather all over the brisket. Refrigerate up to 24 hours if time allows.
  2. ROAST: Preheat oven to 275°F. Place brisket FAT SIDE UP in a large roasting pan. Scatter vegetables around. Pour remaining marinade over. Seal tightly with foil.
  3. Cook 6–8 hours, about 1 hour per pound, until meat pulls apart easily.
  4. COOL & SLICE: Refrigerate overnight in the cooking liquid. Remove solidified fat. Slice against the grain.
  5. REHEAT: Place sliced meat in pan fat-side down, cover tightly, warm at 350°F until heated through.
  6. FREEZER OPTION: Freeze cooled sliced meat in the sauce. Reheat from frozen at 350°F, covered.

Notes & Tips

  • Buy the brisket whole with the fat cap on — fat side UP.
  • Foil pans are ideal: easy cleanup, easy freezing.
  • Low and slow (275°F) is the key.
  • Freezer-encouraged: Make and freeze up to 2 weeks ahead.

Julia's Favorite Roast Chicken ↗

Yield: 4–5 servings | Active Time: 25 min | Total Time: ~1 hr 30 min

Julia Child's classic Poulet Rôti — buttery, basted, deeply flavored. Turn and baste throughout for the juiciest result.

Ingredients

Steps

  1. Remove chicken from fridge 30–45 min before cooking. Preheat oven to 425°F.
  2. Melt 1 tbsp butter in a skillet. Add carrot, onion, and celery; cook over medium ~5 min. Add thyme.
  3. Pat chicken dry. Season cavity with ¼ tsp salt, smear in 1 tbsp butter. Stuff with vegetables, parsley stems, celery leaves, and lemon slices. Truss the legs.
  4. Melt remaining 1 tbsp butter with the cooking oil — this is your basting liquid.
  5. Place chicken breast-side up in a shallow roasting pan. Pour basting liquid over. Roast on the middle rack.
  6. At 15 min: baste and turn onto one side. Salt lightly.
  7. At 40 min: baste and turn onto the other side. Baste every 8–10 min.
  8. 15 min before done: salt again, turn breast-side up. Total time ~65–75 min; internal temp 165°F at thickest thigh.
  9. Rest 15 min. PAN SAUCE: Spoon off fat, add lemon juice then stock. Boil, scraping up browned bits. Simmer ~5 min. Strain and spoon over each serving.

Notes & Tips

  • Don't skip bringing the chicken to room temp.
  • Baste every 8–10 minutes — don’t leave the kitchen.

Lemon & Garlic Baked Chicken Thighs ↗

Yield: 4–6 servings | Total Time: ~45 min

Foolproof weeknight chicken — bone-in thighs marinated in lemon, garlic, and olive oil, seared until crispy, finished in the oven.

Ingredients

Steps

  1. Whisk lemon juice, zest, garlic, 2 tbsp oil, brown sugar, salt, and pepper. Marinate chicken 15–30 min at room temp (or up to 8 hrs refrigerated).
  2. Preheat oven to 400°F.
  3. Heat remaining 1 tbsp oil in a large oven-safe skillet over medium-high. Sear chicken skin-side down until golden and crispy, 6–8 min.
  4. Flip chicken, transfer skillet to oven. Roast until 165°F internally, 18–22 min.
  5. Rest 5 minutes. Garnish with lemon slices and fresh thyme.

Notes & Tips

  • Don't skip the sear — it's what gives you crispy skin.
  • Works with boneless thighs; reduce oven time by ~5 min.

Simple Marinated Grilled Chicken Thighs ↗

Yield: 4 servings | Marinate: 30 min–12 hrs | Grill: ~15 min

Boneless chicken thighs in a punchy soy-fish sauce-lemongrass marinade, grilled fast and finished with lime. An enormous weeknight hit. ★ Family tested and approved.

Ingredients

Marinade

To Serve

Steps

  1. MARINADE: Whisk together soy sauce, fish sauce, oil, brown sugar, lemon zest (or lemongrass), garlic, ginger, and salt in a large bowl.
  2. Add chicken thighs and toss to coat well. Cover and marinate at room temperature for 30 minutes, or refrigerate up to 12 hours.
  3. GRILL: Prepare a grill for medium-high heat; lightly oil the grates. Remove chicken from marinade, letting any excess drip off.
  4. Grill chicken, turning once, until deep grill marks form and chicken is cooked through, about 5–6 minutes per side. Transfer to a cutting board and let rest 5 minutes.
  5. Slice or serve whole. Finish with a generous squeeze of lime juice over everything. Scatter cilantro over the top. Serve with remaining lime wedges on the side.

Notes & Tips

  • The original recipe uses lemongrass (finely grated or minced) — lemon zest is an excellent swap that’s easier to find. Both give bright, citrusy lift.
  • Don’t skip the lime wedges at the end — the acid is what makes the dish pop. Keep them on the ingredient list so you remember to buy them.
  • Fish sauce is key here — it adds depth without tasting fishy. Don’t substitute.
  • Boneless thighs cook fast and stay juicy. Watch the grill — the sugar in the marinade can cause flare-ups.
  • ★ Family-tested weeknight winner. Also great the next day sliced cold over rice or in a lettuce wrap.
  • Also appears in: Weeknight Dinner Menu section.

Salmon with Brown Sugar and Bourbon Glaze

Source: no online link on file

Yield: 4 servings | Total Time: ~20 min

Butter, brown sugar, and bourbon caramelize into a rich lacquered glaze. Fast, impressive, and foolproof.

Ingredients

Steps

  1. Melt butter in a large heavy skillet over medium heat. Stir in brown sugar until dissolved.
  2. Place salmon on top of the brown sugar mixture. Cook 5 minutes without moving.
  3. Flip salmon. Pour bourbon around (not over) the fillets. Cook another 5 minutes, or until fish flakes easily.

Yield: 4 servings (8 brats) | Total Time: ~1 hr

The real deal — charcoal grilled low and slow for the snap, then finished in a buttery beer bath. Always served as a double on a hard roll.

Ingredients

The Beer Bath (Hot Tub)

The Grill

The Build

Steps

Roasted Tomato Soup ↗

Yield: 6–8 servings | Active Time: 20 min | Roast: 1 hr

Roasting the tomatoes and a whole head of garlic does all the work. Sweet, deep, and creamy. Serve with grilled cheese.

Ingredients

Steps

  1. Preheat oven to 300°F. In a Dutch oven, sauté onion in ¼ cup olive oil until golden, 5–10 min. Add salt; cook 3–5 more min.

Artisan Bread (à la Wilburn) ↗

Yield: 1 loaf | Active Time: 30 min | Rise: 1.5–2 hrs (or overnight) | Bake: 45 min

Ingredients

Steps

  1. Stir flour, yeast, and salt together. Add water; stir until very sticky. Cover and place in a warm spot until doubled, 1½–2 hours. (Or refrigerate 8–24 hours.)
  2. Preheat oven to 450°F with a large Dutch oven (and lid) inside for at least 30 minutes.
  3. If refrigerated, rest dough 15 minutes at room temperature.
  4. With floured hands, stretch and fold: reach down one side, pull up, fold over center. Turn bowl 90° and repeat, ~8–10 folds total. Pinch seams.
  5. Shape into a round. Transfer to parchment paper, dust with flour, cover loosely 10 minutes.
  6. Score the top. Lower dough (with parchment) into the hot Dutch oven. Cover with lid.
  7. Bake covered 30 min. Remove lid and bake until deeply golden, 15–17 more min. Cool at least 20 min before slicing.

Notes & Tips

  • Check water temp — too hot kills yeast; too cold won’t activate it.
  • Overnight fridge rise develops deeper flavor.
  • Measure flour by weight if possible.

Matzoh Brei à la Mama

Source: Family recipe — no online link on file

Yield: 1 serving (scales easily) | Total Time: ~10 min

A simple, comforting Passover breakfast — matzo soaked soft and scrambled with egg in butter. Listen for the squeak; that’s how you know it’s ready.

Ingredients

Steps

  1. Break the matzo into small pieces in a bowl and soak in not-quite-hot water until soft but not mushy, about 1–2 minutes.
  2. Drain in a strainer, pressing out excess moisture.
  3. Beat the egg well and return the matzo to the bowl, mixing thoroughly with the beaten egg.
  4. Melt butter in a medium skillet over medium-high heat.
  5. Add the matzo mixture to the hot skillet; season with salt and pepper to taste. Let cook 1–2 minutes until the mixture sets, then reduce heat and scramble as you would scramble eggs until desired doneness. Listen for the “squeak” as you scramble — when it squeaks, it’s ready!

Notes & Tips

  • For additional servings, use approximately the same matzo-to-egg ratio; 2 sheets of matzo per person may need an additional egg.
  • It takes more salt than you might think — but you can always add more after cooking.
